## Material differences summarised

* Legacy decides fills from full-day bars (`low/high/amount/close`) at an implied open; the kernel requires evidence observed and available at the attempt instant, or a declared assumption that is visibly flagged.
* Legacy records a trade before checking cash; the kernel checks cash (with all charges) before recording anything.
* Legacy counts holding/settlement in natural days over bar dates; the kernel counts exchange sessions on an injected calendar and keeps halted sessions.
* Legacy carries unversioned settings rates and a literal minimum commission with no transfer fee; the kernel takes an effective-dated, versioned, provenance-labelled schedule and has no defaults.
* Legacy infers board/ST from the code and treats unknown as normal; the kernel rejects unknown state unless explicitly assumed.
* Legacy has no order expiry, no role check and float/4-decimal prices; the kernel has explicit expiry, a role check and Decimal tick-aligned prices.

What was **not** established: how often item 5 (fill recorded without cash) or item 2 (close-dependent sizing) actually changes a legacy run — that needs a controlled reproduction, which is later work under a separate task, not a claim made here.
